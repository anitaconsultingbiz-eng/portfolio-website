Data organization and planning

Course Tracking and Enrollment Forecasting

The need
Program leadership needed a reliable way to anticipate which online courses students would require in future semesters.
What made it difficult
Student plans, curriculum requirements, course schedules, and enrollment information lived in separate records. A change to one student’s plan could affect future demand.
My responsibility
I determined what information needed to be connected, designed the tracking structure, consolidated the data, and maintained the system for ongoing planning.
What I produced
An Excel-based planning system, a consolidated dataset, a repeatable Power Query process, and course-demand forecasts.
The result
Leadership had earlier visibility into projected enrollment and could make better-informed decisions about course schedules, seat capacity, and faculty needs for programs serving more than 300 students each semester.

Tools: Excel, Power Query, Colleague UI, Navigate, Slate

Project and grant tracking

Grant and Initiative Tracking

The need
A small nonprofit needed one dependable place to monitor grant activity, proposals, initiatives, reporting requirements, and follow-up.
What made it difficult
Information was spread across files and conversations. Leadership could not quickly see what was active, what was due, or what needed attention.
My responsibility
I organized the requirements, designed the tracking structure, developed reporting views, and used the system while managing a $100,000 grant through implementation and closeout.
What I produced
A grant portfolio tracker, initiative dashboard, deadline and deliverable log, follow-up record, and leadership reporting view.
The result
The organization gained a practical source of truth for active work, upcoming requirements, and leadership updates.

Tools: Excel, dashboards, shared documentation

Documentation and training

Data Collector Documentation and Training Package

The need
A growing field-work program needed clear materials to prepare Data Collectors, explain professional expectations, and document how users move through a new portal.
What made it difficult
The information came from subject-matter conversations, system screens, business requirements, and an evolving portal. Different audiences needed different levels of instruction.
My responsibility
I reviewed the available systems and source material, documented the user journey, identified unanswered workflow questions, organized the content, and developed practical materials for administrators and new users.
What I produced
A portal manual, professional field guide, workflow documentation, onboarding material, participant tools, and training resources.
The result
Scattered technical and operational information became a structured package that could support onboarding, consistent expectations, and day-to-day portal use.

Tools: Google Workspace, Microsoft Word, portal screenshots, workflow records

Digital workflow

Client Review Workspace

The need
A client project needed a clearer way to review deliverables, provide decisions, and keep feedback from becoming scattered across messages.
What made it difficult
Several deliverables were moving at once, responses were delayed, and there was no shared view of what had been reviewed, approved, or still needed attention.
My responsibility
I translated the communication problem into a practical review workflow, designed the client and administrative views, tested the experience, corrected navigation and status issues, and documented how the workspace should be used.
What I produced
A private client review page, structured feedback forms, status tracking, an administrative dashboard, project and client records, and shareable client links.
The result
Deliverables, review status, and client feedback could be handled in one organized workflow instead of being reconstructed from separate communications.

Tools: Google Apps Script, Google Sheets, HTML, CSS, JavaScript
